    How a data pull gets its priority

    Every data pull is scored on five things: how soon it is needed, how big the opportunity is, how likely the campaign is to succeed, whether our data can actually deliver it, and how much work it takes. The first four add up to the pull's value. Dividing by effort gives its priority, so high-value, low-effort work rises to the top. You can still drag rows to override the order.

    In one line: Priority = (Due-date score + Opportunity ÷ 2 + Campaign success ÷ 2 + Signal ÷ 2) ÷ Effort. Value = the same top part ÷ 4.

    1. Due date score 1–5, set by the deadline

    Why it matters: the nearer the deadline, the more urgent the work. Overdue items score the maximum.

    Time leftScore
    3 days or less, or overdue5
    4–7 days4
    8–14 days3
    15–30 days2
    31+ days, or no deadline1

    2. Opportunity score 1–10, calculated from the form

    Why it matters: how much business this pull could lead to. It adds points for five questions, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 10.

    QuestionAnswerPoints
    What do we expect the client to do?Exploratory only0
    Small test campaign1
    Planned campaign2
    Large or live flight3
    Signs up for Latis4
    How big is the client?Small0
    Mid-size1
    Large2
    How strong is the relationship?First meeting0
    2–3 meetings1
    4 or more meetings2
    Have we done pulls for them before?Yes+1
    Can the work be reused for other clients or verticals?Yes+1

    Example: a large client (2) with 4+ meetings (2) who is expected to sign up for Latis (4), where we have done pulls before (1) and the work is reusable (1) scores 10.

    3. Campaign success score 1–10, AI-scored or set by you

    Why it matters: a big opportunity is only worth chasing if the campaign is likely to work. This score answers: if we run it, will it perform?

    Pressing Score with AI sends the embeddings description to Claude. Claude researches the company on the web (what it sells, who it markets to, how and where it advertises, how big its marketing is) and judges how well the audience fits. It returns a score and a written reason. We also give it the Signal check below, so it knows what our data can support. You can change the score and the reason at any time.

    ScoreMeaning
    8–10Strong fit. Clear audience, sophisticated advertiser, good fit with TV and streaming.
    5–7Reasonable fit with some open questions.
    1–4Weak fit: unclear audience, small or inactive marketer, or poor match to the data.

    4. Signal strength score 1–10, calculated from our data

    Why it matters: it tells us whether we can actually build this audience, and how strong the data behind it is. It is checked against two internal files: the Domain Mapping File (about 8,600 websites, each with a category, intent level and household reach) and the Latis Data Dictionary (the 25 Latis verticals and about 8,350 destinations with reach). Pick a vertical; the key domains (the brand, its competitors, enthusiast sites) come from the linked write-up.

    CheckWhat we look atPoints
    Starting pointEvery pull starts here1
    Vertical qualityShare of that vertical's websites that carry usable signal. 80% or more: 3. 40–79%: 2. Under 40%: 1 (mostly infrastructure or broad sites). Partly listed verticals max out at 2.1–3
    Domain coverageHow many of your domains we actually have. 80% or more: 3. 50–79%: 2. Some: 1. None: 00–3
    ReachTypical share of households that visit those domains. 5% or more: 2. 1–5%: 1. Under 1%: 00–2
    IntentAt least half the domains are marked high intent+1

    Hard limits:

    • Excluded verticals (for example Healthcare and Adult Content, or sensitive and gambling classes) score 1 and are marked "not doable", because Latis does not use that signal.
    • If any domain falls in an excluded class, the score is capped at 3.
    • If no domains are entered, the score is capped at 5, since we cannot confirm coverage.

    Example: Automotive, with Toyota, Honda and Edmunds all in our data, about 10% typical household reach and all high intent, scores 10. A Technology pull with mostly infrastructure sites scores around 6.

    5. Effort score 1–5, calculated; 5 = most work

    Why it matters: it is the cost side. A pull that needs many segments and special rules takes longer, so it should earn its place.

    What adds workPoints
    Starting point1
    2–3 segments+1
    4 or more segments+2
    Overlays needed (Epsilon, Experian, etc.)+1
    Conquest rule: audience must not visit the brand's own site+1
    Win-back rule: audience used to visit the brand's site, then stopped+1

    Capped at 5.

    Putting it together

    Value = (Due-date score + Opportunity ÷ 2 + Campaign success ÷ 2 + Signal ÷ 2) ÷ 4. Opportunity, Success and Signal are halved so every part sits on the same 1–5 scale. Priority = the same top part ÷ Effort.

    Worked example. Due in 5 days (4), Opportunity 8 (4), Campaign success 7 (3.5), Signal 9 (4.5) and Effort 3. The top part is 4 + 4 + 3.5 + 4.5 = 16. Value = 16 ÷ 4 = 4.0. Priority = 16 ÷ 3 = 5.3. High value, low effort: a Do First pull.
